      How to set out an answer

      Each sample is asked the same way. Identify the jar first, then give the other three in this order.

      1. NameThe crude drug and the part used, e.g. Senna leaves, Ipecac root, Ginger rhizomes.
      2. Active principleThe constituent responsible for the effect, e.g. Emetine, Strychnine, Tannic acid.
      3. ActionThe pharmacological group, e.g. Purgative, Expectorant, Carminative.
      4. Therapeutic useWhat it treats, e.g. Treatment of constipation, of bronchitis, of flatulent colic.

      Marking. Each of the four parts is worth one mark. A sample is Correct at 3.5 out of 4 or more (small spelling slips are fine; a part with two ideas, like “arthritis, tendinitis”, gives half for each), Partly right when at least two parts are right, and Wrong otherwise. You can always change the mark yourself.

      Easily confused

      Pairs that share an active principle, a look or a group, and how to keep them apart.

      Senna or Rhubarb

      Both purgatives with the same active principles, emodin and chrysophanic acid. Senna is thin, pale green to brown leaflets; rhubarb is a heavy, yellow-orange rhizome.

      Krameria or Catechu

      Both astringents for diarrhoea, both rich in tannic acid. Krameria is a reddish-brown root; catechu is a dark brown, brittle dried extract in blocks.

      Jaborandi or Calabar beans

      Both parasympathomimetic, both alternate with atropine and treat glaucoma. Jaborandi leaves give pilocarpine (direct acting); Calabar beans give eserine, physostigmine (anti-cholinesterase).

      Croton seeds or Castor seeds

      Two oily seeds with very different jobs. Croton oil is a counter irritant for arthritis and tendinitis; castor oil is a purgative for constipation. Castor seeds are glossy and mottled.

      Squill or Strophanthus

      Both cardiac tonics and secondary diuretics for CHF. Squill is a fleshy bulb (scillarin); Strophanthus is a seed with a feathery tuft (strophanthin).

      Polygala, Ipecac or Eucalyptus

      All three expectorants for bronchitis. Two are roots (senegin, emetine); eucalyptus is leaves, and its oil is also a respiratory tract antiseptic.

      Cinnamon bark or Cinchona bark

      Both barks. Cinnamon is a carminative (cinnamon oil, flatulent colic); cinchona is a stomachic (quinidine, loss of appetite).

      Gentian, Quassia or Columba

      All stomachics and appetisers. Match the bitter to its plant: gentiamarine from gentian root, quassin from quassia wood, columbamine from columba root.

      Linseed or Colocynth

      Both purgatives. Linseed is small, flat, glossy brown seeds (lin oil); colocynth is a dried round gourd fruit (colocynthin).
